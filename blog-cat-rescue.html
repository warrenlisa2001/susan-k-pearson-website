Cat Rescue & Healing · Susan's Story

Why Healing Humans and Rescuing Cats Are the Same Work

Over hundreds of rescued street cats and years of healing practice, Susan has come to understand that compassion cannot be compartmentalised.

She did not plan to become a cat rescuer. It found her, the way the most important things tend to.

And before we go any further — if you are not a cat person, please keep reading. The cats do not come into the session. Your healing space is entirely feline-free. Susan simply asks that you extend them the same dignity she extends to every living creature that walks through her life. That is all.

(Though if you are a cat person, be warned: you may leave your session with a new family member. Consider yourself informed.)

It Started Long Before Dubai

Susan's relationship with animals did not begin in the UAE. It began in childhood — in the quiet, instinctive way that deeply empathic children find themselves drawn to creatures in need. While other children played, Susan was the one bringing home injured birds, sitting with frightened strays, pressing her small hands gently against an animal's side and willing it to be well.

There was something in her, even then, that understood the language of a nervous system in distress. She did not have words for it as a child. She simply knew that presence — quiet, patient, unhurried presence — could do something that nothing else could. That it could reach a creature who had retreated entirely into fear and say: you are safe now. I am not going anywhere.

This empathic attunement would eventually become the foundation of her healing practice. But as a child, it simply meant that the animals found her. They still do.

Jessie — the unofficial Head of Household and self-appointed cat caretaker.

And Then There Is Jessie

No account of Susan's rescue work would be complete without mentioning Jessie — her Westie, her companion, and what can only be described as the most enthusiastic, non-feline member of the cat rescue operation.

Jessie has, over the years, taken it upon herself to become a fully certified cat caretaker. New arrivals — often terrified, hissing, backed into corners — are greeted by Jessie with a combination of gentle curiosity and complete indifference to their dramatic protests. She does not take it personally. She simply settles nearby and waits. In this, she is perhaps the best teacher of patience Susan has ever had.

Kittens, in particular, have a habit of attaching themselves to Jessie with great enthusiasm. Jessie tolerates this with the dignity of someone who has learned that love, in all its forms, is occasionally inconvenient and always worth it.

Jessie with one of the rescued kittens — a friendship that required no translation.

The Streets of Dubai

Dubai's streets are home to thousands of cats — animals that survive on the edges of human infrastructure, often injured, sick, or malnourished. They are largely invisible to most people. Susan sees them. She has always seen them.

Over the years she has built a network of volunteers, veterinarians, and foster families across the UAE. She funds a significant portion of the rescue work herself — vet bills, food, medication, and the ongoing logistics of getting cats from the streets into safe and loving homes. She has sat with injured animals through the night. She has said goodbye to cats she loved deeply and celebrated the ones who found families and thrived.

A rescued mother with her kittens — given safety, she blossomed.

One of hundreds given a second chance.

Beo and Foxy — a bonded pair rescued from the streets of Dubai. They arrived terrified and inseparable. They left the same way, only now they had a home.

Reiki for Animals

It might sound unusual. It shouldn't. Animals have nervous systems. They carry trauma. They hold tension in their bodies. They shut down when they don't feel safe, and they open when they do. They respond to energy — anyone who has ever had a cat choose to sit on them during a difficult day already understands this intuitively.

Susan has worked with rescued cats who arrived in states of profound physical and emotional collapse — animals whose bodies were shutting down, who had stopped eating, who had retreated so far into themselves that conventional care alone was not reaching them. She would sit quietly with them, offering Reiki — not imposing it, never forcing, simply making it available — and watch something begin to change.

The breath would slow. The muscles would release their rigid grip. An eye might open. A paw might uncurl. Over sessions, animals that veterinarians had feared might not survive began to eat again, to engage, to remember that life was worth returning to.

"Every animal that has been abandoned carries a story. My job is not to erase that story but to give it a new beginning."

— Susan K. Pearson

The Connection Between Healing and Rescue

People sometimes ask Susan how she holds both — the healing practice and the rescue work. The honest answer is that for her, they are not two things. They are the same impulse wearing different clothes.

In her healing room, she meets human beings who are frightened, shut down, disconnected from their own bodies — often as a result of experiences that told them the world was not safe. In her rescue work, she meets animals in exactly the same state. The nervous system does not distinguish between species. The need for safety, attunement, and patient presence is universal.

What heals a traumatised cat is the same thing that heals a traumatised human: consistent safety, unhurried presence, and the quiet message delivered through action rather than words — you are seen, you matter, and you are not alone.

"A frightened cat will not be rushed into trust. Neither will a nervous system. You create the conditions and you wait. The opening always comes."

— Susan K. Pearson

A Portion of Every Session Goes Toward the Rescue

Susan has made a quiet commitment: a portion of every session contributes directly to the rescue work. To vet bills, food, medical care, and the ongoing work of getting cats from the streets into safe, loving homes. When you book a session with Susan, you are not only investing in your own healing — you are participating in something larger.

Compassion, in Susan's world, is not selective. It does not stop at the door of the healing room. And if you need a reminder of that — Jessie is always nearby to demonstrate it.

How You Can Help

If you are in Dubai or the UAE and would like to adopt, foster, or support the work financially, Susan welcomes your contact. There are always cats who need temporary foster homes while they await permanent placement. There are always vet bills. And there are always animals who simply need someone to notice them.

And if you are still firmly in the non-cat-person camp — that is absolutely fine. Book a session. Experience the healing. Know that somewhere, quietly, a small street cat is also having a better day because you did.

The work of rescue, like the work of healing, begins with a single act of attention. You see something. You choose not to look away. Everything follows from that.
