The Science Behind Energy Healing

An exploration of what modern research tells us about biofields, energy medicine, and why ancient practices have found a new home in contemporary wellness.

By Susan K. Pearson  ·  June 2026  ·  10 min read

When people first encounter the idea of energy healing, a common response is scepticism. And that scepticism is reasonable. We live in an era shaped by evidence-based medicine, and anything that cannot be measured in a clinical trial can feel suspect. But the conversation around biofields, energy medicine and the subtle workings of the human body has grown more sophisticated — and more scientifically grounded — than many people realise.

This article is not a claim that energy healing is magic. It is an invitation to look more carefully at what we already know about the body, and to consider why practices like Reiki, hands-on healing and energy medicine may be doing something measurable — even if we don't yet have the full language to describe it.

The Body as a Field, Not Just a Machine

Western medicine has long treated the body as a biochemical machine: organs, cells, molecules, reactions. This model has produced extraordinary results. But it is not the only framework available.

Biofield science — a growing area of research — proposes that the body generates and responds to electromagnetic and other forms of energy. Every cell in the body produces electrical activity. The heart generates the largest electromagnetic field of any organ, measurable several feet from the body. The brain produces distinct brainwave patterns that can be detected externally. This is not speculative — it is the basis for the ECG and EEG technologies used in hospitals worldwide.

"The question is not whether the body produces energy fields. It does. The question is whether those fields carry information that can influence health — and that is where the research is becoming genuinely interesting."

Researchers at institutions including the National Institutes of Health in the United States have been investigating biofield science for decades. The Consciousness and Healing Initiative, a collaborative of scientists and clinicians, defines the biofield as "a complex, dynamic, weak electromagnetic field surrounding and permeating living organisms." The research suggests this field may play a role in regulating biological processes — from cellular communication to immune function.

What Happens in the Body During Healing Touch

Multiple studies have examined what occurs physiologically when a person receives hands-on energy work. The findings are consistent enough to be worth paying attention to.

Heart Rate Variability (HRV) — a measure of the variation in time between heartbeats, widely considered a marker of nervous system regulation — has been shown to improve following Reiki and therapeutic touch sessions. Higher HRV is associated with greater resilience, better immune function and more effective stress response.

Cortisol levels, the body's primary stress hormone, have been observed to decrease following energy healing sessions. A 2010 study published in the Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine found that Reiki produced significantly greater reductions in anxiety and pre-operative stress than a resting control condition.

Pain reduction is another consistently observed effect. Several hospital-based studies — including work carried out at Yale, Harvard and the Cleveland Clinic — have incorporated Reiki into pain management protocols for surgical patients and those with chronic conditions, reporting meaningful improvements in subjective pain experience.

The Placebo Problem — And Why It Matters Less Than You Think

The sceptic's response to these findings is often: "That's just the placebo effect." And it is worth taking that seriously — rather than dismissing it.

The placebo effect is one of the most powerful and least understood phenomena in medicine. The expectation of healing, the therapeutic relationship, the felt sense of being cared for — these produce measurable physiological changes. Immune markers shift. Pain pathways are modulated. Healing accelerates.

"If a treatment reliably produces healing — even partly through expectation and relational attunement — then the question 'is it the placebo?' becomes less important than the question 'is it working?'"

Susan would add something to this from clinical experience: the people who arrive most sceptical are often among those who feel the most during a session. The body doesn't require belief to respond. It requires presence, safety and contact — which is what skilled energy work provides.

The Nervous System: The Real Frontier

Perhaps the most compelling scientific framework for understanding energy healing is not quantum physics or biofield theory — it is the autonomic nervous system.

We now understand, far more clearly than we did even twenty years ago, that the nervous system is not just a communication network. It is the body's primary organising system — governing not only fight-or-flight responses but the regulation of organs, immune function, digestion, hormonal balance and tissue repair. When the nervous system is chronically dysregulated — as it is in most people navigating modern life — the effects cascade through every system of the body.

Energy healing works, in part, by creating conditions for nervous system regulation. The quality of presence in a skilled practitioner — calm, attuned, unhurried — has a measurable co-regulatory effect on the client's nervous system. This is not metaphor. It is the science of co-regulation, demonstrated clearly in the work of researchers like Stephen Porges, whose Polyvagal Theory has transformed how we understand the social nervous system.

Ancient Wisdom, Modern Context

Practices like Reiki, acupuncture, hands-on healing and energy medicine are ancient. They arose in cultures that did not have electron microscopes or fMRI machines — but they were developed by careful observers of the human body over thousands of years of practice. The fact that we are now beginning to find scientific correlates for what these systems describe is not a vindication of superstition. It is the arrival of science at a place that human experience already knew.

Susan holds both. She is a practitioner grounded in tradition, training and decades of hands-on experience. She is also someone who reads the research, who finds the science genuinely fascinating, and who welcomes the questions that scepticism brings.

You don't need to believe in energy healing for it to work. You do need to show up, lie down, and let the body do what it already knows how to do.
